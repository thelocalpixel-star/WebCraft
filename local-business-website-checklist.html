A practical guide for East Tennessee businesses

A local business website checklist

Your website should help someone understand what you offer, decide whether it’s right for them, and take the next step. These basics are a good place to start.

Whether your customers are in Cleveland, Chattanooga, Athens, Knoxville, or the surrounding area, the same principle applies: use accurate details about your business and make them easy to find.

1. Explain what you do, and where

Put your primary service and the places you serve near the top of the page. Write for a customer who has never heard of you. If you don’t receive visitors at a public location, describe your service area instead of implying there’s an office in every city.

Give each important service a clear description. A landscaping company, for example, could explain whether it offers maintenance, installation, or both, rather than just saying “we do it all.”

2. Make the next step obvious

Choose the action that makes sense for your business: call, request an estimate, visit a shop, or book an appointment. Put a clear link or button where it’s easy to see. Test phone links and forms on a phone, and tell customers what to expect after they get in touch.

3. Show evidence customers can inspect

Use real work samples, original photographs where you have permission, and details that help people evaluate your service. If you display reviews, make sure they come from real customers and reflect their actual experience. A specific project description can be more useful than a generic claim that you’re “the best.”

4. Check the details on mobile

  • Read each page on a phone without zooming in.
  • Check that buttons are easy to tap and forms have clear labels.
  • Confirm your phone, email, hours, and address or service area are accurate.
  • Remove outdated offers and broken links.
  • Make sure images support the page without slowing it down unnecessarily.

5. Help search engines discover your site

Use descriptive page titles, helpful headings, and links that visitors can follow. A sitemap can help Google discover pages; its sitemap guidance explains how to submit one. A verified Google Search Console property lets you inspect pages and review search queries after data becomes available.

For businesses eligible for a Google Business Profile, keep the profile accurate and consistent with the website. Read Google’s business profile guidelines before choosing an address or service area. Google determines search visibility; no website change guarantees a ranking.

Before you launch

Ask someone unfamiliar with your business to open the site on a phone. Can they tell what you do, where you work, and how to reach you in a few seconds? Their answer will reveal what to clarify.
