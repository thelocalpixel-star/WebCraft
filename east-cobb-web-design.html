A community without a city hall

East Cobb has no mayor, no city limits, and no downtown of its own. It is an unincorporated part of Cobb County, home to about 164,000 people in the Northeast Cobb census area, and most residents hold a Marietta address even though they live outside the City of Marietta. In May 2022, voters rejected a proposal to make East Cobb a city, with about 73 percent voting no.

For a business, that shape matters. There is no single town center where customers gather, and no city name that neatly describes the market. Customers find businesses by corridor and by neighborhood — Johnson Ferry Road, Roswell Road, the Avenue East Cobb, the streets off Lower Roswell Road — and the website has to name those places, because the search does. A site that says only Marietta is telling half the story, and a site that claims an East Cobb office it does not have is telling a false one.

The five pages an East Cobb business needs

WebCraft's standard website covers up to five pages for $500, one time, and five pages are enough for most small businesses here — if each page has a job. Home says what the business does and where, in the first screen. Services says what it sells, one page per service it actually sells. About says who is behind it. Contact says how to reach a person. The fifth page earns its place by answering the question customers actually ask.

For an East Cobb business, the area page matters most. Name the corridors and neighborhoods served — Johnson Ferry, Roswell Road, the streets your customers actually live on — and say plainly that the work is done remotely or on site, whichever is true. A customer searching from East Cobb wants to know the business serves East Cobb, not that it has a building there.

Say where you are without claiming a city you are not in

The mailing address and the market are different things in East Cobb, and the website should say so honestly. A Marietta mailing address does not make a business a Marietta business, and an East Cobb business does not need a Marietta address to serve East Cobb customers. Say the area served, name the roads and neighborhoods, and let the customer decide — that is more convincing than a borrowed city name.

The same honesty applies to the whole site. WebCraft works remotely, with no office in East Cobb and no office in Atlanta; the project runs by phone, email, and shared drafts, and the price is the same $500 whether the business sits on Johnson Ferry Road or in Roswell. A business that is honest about where it is will be believed about everything else on the page.

The remote project, in order

A remote project runs in the same order as a local one, without the meetings. First the brief: what the business does, who it serves, and what the site must do. Then a first draft of the homepage, shared as a link the owner can open on a phone. Then revisions — usually two rounds — on the words, the photos, and the details. Then launch, and a handover: the domain in the owner's name, the hosting account the owner can reach, and the files the owner keeps.

The whole project usually takes days, not months, for a five-page site, and the owner reviews every step from their own phone or computer. There is no drive to an office and no waiting room; there is a link, a call, and a draft. If a provider cannot show you a draft on a link, or cannot say who holds the domain, that is the warning sign — remote or not.

What to check before you hire anyone

Before hiring any web designer, ask three questions and get the answers in writing. Who owns the domain name and the site when the work is done? What does the price include, and what costs extra — the domain, the hosting, the photos, the pages beyond five? And what happens if you want to leave: can you take the site, the domain, and the content with you, and what does that cost?

Compare the answers, not the slogans. A $500 site you own outright, with hosting and care at $20 a month only if you want it, is a different offer from a cheap build that rents you the site by the month and switches it off when you stop paying. East Cobb businesses have plenty of choice — the right choice is the one that leaves the business holding its own website, its own name, and its own customers.
