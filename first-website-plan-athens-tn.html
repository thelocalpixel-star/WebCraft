A First Website Plan for an Athens Small Business

Plan a first small business website in Athens around essential service information and a clear contact route. Choose the page structure, mobile layout and content before committing to a website design package.

Choose the questions your first site must answer

Before you choose colors, list the questions you answer before a customer hires you. Most first websites need to explain the offer, establish who is behind it and give a dependable way to make contact. A business in Athens can begin with those essentials instead of commissioning features it has no process to support. Give each planned page a specific job.

Put it into practice

Write one sentence for your homepage, services, about, work examples and contact page. If two pages have the same purpose, combine them before paying to build both.

Gather a small, usable content kit

Collect your current service descriptions, approved photographs, logo and monitored contact details. Ask someone unfamiliar with your business to read the material. Can they explain what you sell and what they should do next? That exercise often identifies missing information sooner than a design review. Avoid borrowing photographs that imply work you did not perform.

Put it into practice

Put the content in one folder with descriptive filenames. Mark anything that still needs permission or fact-checking so it cannot be mistaken for final copy.

Price the first year, then agree on the launch

Separate the website build from its recurring costs. Ask whether the proposal includes writing, domain registration, hosting and later edits. WebCraft’s five-page build is $500; optional hosting and care are $20 a month. That combination is $740 for twelve months before domains, additional pages or third-party charges. Confirm the particular scope in writing.

Put it into practice

Name the person approving the draft, the inbox receiving inquiries and the accounts you will control. Test the site on a phone before accepting delivery.

Your planning checklist

Use these prompts to prepare a brief or review the work with your provider. Record the decision, the person responsible and the point at which it will be checked.

  • Essential pages → purpose of each page
  • Content folder → owner and permission status
  • First-year cost → build plus recurring fees

Resolve the items that affect a customer’s ability to understand the service or make contact first. Then agree on the remaining scope. A longer feature list is not automatically a better fit for your business.
