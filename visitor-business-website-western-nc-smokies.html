A Practical Website Checklist for Visitor-Facing Businesses

Plan clear mobile pages, photography, current information and booking handoffs for visitor-facing businesses in western North Carolina and the Smokies.

By WebCraft · Published November 4, 2026 · Planning guide

Answer the first visit questions

A first-time customer has less background than a regular. Explain what your business offers, where the service happens, what is included and the next step. If customers visit a physical location, publish accurate directions and hours.

This checklist is intended for businesses serving first-time visitors in communities such as Asheville, Murphy and the Smokies. It does not assume your business offers accommodation, tours or online booking; include only the information relevant to your actual service.

Choose photographs that explain the experience

Use images that help someone understand the real place, product or service. Add concise captions where a photograph could be misunderstood. Make sure you have permission to publish the images and any identifiable customers.

Avoid presenting a stock image as your own property or completed work. Strong photography earns attention most effectively when it sets an accurate expectation.

Keep the mobile path short

Make important details easy to scan before the visitor reaches a form or booking tool. Test navigation, text size, tap targets and the clarity of your main action on a narrow screen.

Long decorative sections can push practical details out of reach. Give the page a clear hierarchy: understand the offer, check the relevant details, then take the next step.

Explain third-party booking handoffs

If you already use a booking or payment provider, explain what happens when a visitor follows that link. Check that the destination is correct and that the page does not imply availability or prices that the provider no longer offers.

A click to a booking service is not proof that a booking was completed. Use the measurement options supported by that provider and keep click reporting separate from confirmed reservations or purchases.

Make updates part of the plan

Assign someone to check hours, seasonal information, services and outbound links. Review the pages after a meaningful business change rather than relying on a launch-day check forever.

If you serve multiple communities, use the website to explain the actual service and relevant differences. Avoid publishing several nearly identical pages that leave visitors with the same unanswered questions.
