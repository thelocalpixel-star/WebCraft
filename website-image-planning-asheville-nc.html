An Image Planning Guide for Asheville Business Websites

Choose business website photos that explain your offer, protect customer privacy and load sensibly on mobile.

By WebCraft · Published November 4, 2026 · Practical business guide

In this guideChoose the message before choosing the imageSeparate original work from illustrationPrepare the image for its actual placementYour planning checklist

Choose the message before choosing the image

A photograph can introduce a place, demonstrate a product or show the details of a service. Decide which of those jobs each image needs to do. For an Asheville business planning a site, a consistent photo brief makes the design more coherent than a folder assembled from unrelated visual styles.

Put it into practice

List the questions a visitor might ask about the offer. Match each priority question to an image that helps answer it and a caption supplying any missing context.

Separate original work from illustration

Do not let a stock photo imply that you own a property or completed a project. Get permission for customer images and recognizable people. Use concepts as concepts. A polished presentation depends on the accuracy of its details as much as the quality of the photography.

Put it into practice

Keep a record of image origin, permission and permitted uses. If you cannot establish a right to use a picture, replace it before publication.

Prepare the image for its actual placement

A wide desktop hero and a narrow phone view may need different crops. Preserve the subject and leave space for any surrounding copy. Supply appropriately sized files and descriptive alternative text where the image conveys information. Alternative text should explain the picture, not repeat a list of target cities.

Put it into practice

Review the page with images loaded and on a slow connection. Make sure the customer can still understand the offer and reach the contact option.

Your planning checklist

Use these prompts to prepare a brief or review the work with your provider. Record the decision, the person responsible and the point at which it will be checked.

  • Image purpose → question it answers
  • Rights → owner and publication permission
  • Mobile crop → subject stays recognizable

Resolve the items that affect a customer’s ability to understand the service or make contact first. Then agree on the remaining scope. A longer feature list is not automatically a better fit for your business.
