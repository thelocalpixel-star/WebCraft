Pausing a Cartersville Google Ads Campaign

A Cartersville campaign that is spending money on the wrong calls should be paused, not left running while the business hopes it improves. Pausing is a decision, not a shrug — and a paused campaign that restarts with one thing changed is worth more than a campaign that ran for three months on hope. This guide is for the Cartersville business that needs to stop the spend, fix the cause, and restart on purpose.

Pause with a reason, written down

Cartersville is the seat of Bartow County, on Interstate 75 about 42 miles northwest of Atlanta, where the Etowah River runs past a downtown built on Main Street — and a Cartersville campaign usually has a local reason to pause. The crew is booked out for three weeks. The season ended.

The calls are coming from Acworth or Emerson, outside the area the business serves. Or the calls are coming in, but they are the wrong kind: job seekers, free estimates for work the business does not do, people who want a price and nothing else.

Write the reason down before you touch the pause button, in one sentence, with the date. A paused campaign without a reason is a campaign nobody restarts, because nobody remembers why it stopped or what would have to change to start it again.

A paused campaign with a written reason is a campaign with a restart condition — and that is the difference between a pause and a quiet abandonment.

What pausing does — and does not — do

Pausing stops the spend immediately: no new impressions, no new clicks, no new charges from that campaign. The history stays — the keywords, the ads, the landing pages, and the numbers from every month it ran — and the campaign can be switched back on in one click.

Nothing about the account is lost, and the business is not starting over when it restarts.

What pausing does not do is fix anything. A campaign paused for bad geography will still have bad geography when it restarts. A campaign paused because the landing page did not convert will still have the same page. And pausing does not refund the money already spent, or the calls already missed.

The pause buys time to fix the cause; it is not the fix itself, and a business that pauses without fixing will pay the same money twice.

Change one thing before you restart

Use the pause to change exactly one thing, and make it the thing the numbers point to. If the calls came from the wrong towns, tighten the map to Cartersville and the Bartow County towns the business actually serves — White, Taylorsville, Euharlee, Emerson — and exclude the rest.

If the clicks were cheap and the calls were few, fix the landing page before touching the keywords. If the search terms show the wrong intent, add the negatives: free, DIY, jobs, how to.

One change matters because a restart with five changes teaches nothing. If the calls improve, the business will not know which change did it, and if they do not improve, it will not know which change to undo.

Change the page, or the map, or the keywords — pick the one the evidence blames, fix it, and let the restart test that fix alone.

The restart checklist

Before the campaign goes live again, run a short checklist. The daily budget cap is set to an amount the business can spend for a month without touching payroll, rent, or fuel. The tracking number is in place, so every call from the ads is counted separately from the main line.

The landing page opens on a phone, shows the service, the area, and the phone number in the first screen, and the form — if there is one — asks for a name, a phone number, and the service, nothing more.

Then restart small and watch the first week closely: how many calls, how many were real inquiries, what each one cost, and where they came from. A restart is a new test, not a return to the old campaign, and the first week's numbers decide whether the fix worked or whether the next change is needed.

If the same problem appears in the first week, pause again — the second pause is cheaper than the second month.

When to stop for good

Some campaigns should not restart at all, and the numbers will say so plainly. If a month of honest testing — one service, one page, the right towns, the negatives in place — brought inquiries that cost more than the jobs they won, the campaign is not working for this business, in this town, at this price.

That is not a failure of effort; it is an answer, and the money should go to the pages and reviews that bring calls without a per-click charge.

Stop the same way you paused: write the reason down, with the numbers beside it, and keep the campaign in the account rather than deleting it. A stopped campaign with a written record is a lesson the business keeps; a deleted one is a lesson it will pay to learn again.

And the decision can change — a new service, a new season, a better page — but the next start should be a new test, not a quiet return to the old spend.
