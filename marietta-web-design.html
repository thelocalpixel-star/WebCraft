What a Marietta customer checks before driving over

Marietta has a real downtown — the Square, with its shops, restaurants and the Strand Theatre — and many of its businesses depend on customers deciding to drive over. That decision is made on a phone, before the drive: is the business open now, where exactly is it, can I park, can I call.

A website that answers those four questions in seconds is a website that brings the visit. A website that buries them is a website the customer leaves for the next result.

The pre-visit check is the test every Marietta site should pass: on a phone, as a customer would, find the hours, the address, the phone number and the directions — every one of them, in under a minute, without hunting.

If any one of them is missing, stale or hard to find, the site is losing visits it never sees: customers who checked, could not find what they needed, and went somewhere that answered.

The five pages a Marietta site needs first

Five pages come first. The homepage says what the business does, where it works, and how to reach it — naming the Square or Route 41, so a visitor driving over knows it is the right place. The service page follows in the customer’s words, not the trade’s, and the about page says who the business is in plain words.

The contact page carries the phone number, a form, and the hours. The fifth page is proof: a real job, a real review in the customer’s own words. A claim without proof is a claim the visitor has no reason to believe, and a Marietta visitor who has three other businesses one tap away will not wait to be convinced.

Five pages done well beat twenty pages of filler, and each page answers one question. The site can grow later — a second service page, a page for East Cobb, a page for the season — but the growth comes after the five, not instead of them.

The mistake is building the twenty pages first and never getting to the five good ones. A visitor who lands on a page that answers them calls. A visitor faced with filler clicks the next result, and the Square has plenty of next results.

The first month after launch: the three checks

Going live is not the finish. In the first month, run three checks, starting with the phone: call the number on the site and see who answers.

Send a test message from the contact form, using a real email address, and confirm the reply arrives. A form that shows a thank-you page and sends nothing is the most common launch-day failure, and only a real test message finds it. Then check that the map listing points at the right place, with the right hours.

Then search for the business name and look at the first page of results. A new site needs time to show up in search — days to weeks before it is indexed, months before it ranks — and nobody can promise it faster.

Write down what was checked and when, and keep the login details with it — hosting account, domain registrar, and the email the account is registered under. A first-month check is a launch the business can trust; a launch never checked is a guess.
