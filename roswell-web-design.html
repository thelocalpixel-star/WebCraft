Roswell searches, Atlanta answers

Searches for web design in Roswell land on Atlanta pages, and that is not a mistake. Roswell is a Fulton County city north of Atlanta, with a historic downtown around Canton Street and a business community that serves Roswell, Alpharetta and the rest of the north metro. The designers serving it work remotely, by phone, email and online reviews — and the pages that answer Roswell searches say so plainly, instead of pretending to have an office on the Square.

A remote designer is not a distant designer. The work — the brief, the draft, the review rounds, the launch — happens online, and the business approves every step from its own office. The question is not whether the designer has a desk in Roswell. It is whether the designer answers the phone, delivers the draft on time, and hands over a site the business owns.

How a remote project works

The project starts with a conversation: the business, the services, the customers, and what the website has to do. Then comes a first draft — the real pages, with real words — for the business to review. Changes are made in rounds, each one agreed before the next begins, and the site launches only when the business approves it. Nothing about that process needs a meeting room.

The review rounds are where remote projects succeed or fail. A business that reviews the draft promptly, with specific notes — this photo is wrong, this sentence says the wrong thing, this page is missing — gets a site that sounds like itself. A business that waits three weeks and then asks for everything to change gets a delay. The designer’s job is to make the review easy; the business’s job is to do it.

The five pages a Roswell business needs

Most Roswell businesses need five pages first: a home page that says what the business does and where, a services page for each main service, an about page with real proof, a contact page with the phone number and the area served, and one page that answers the question customers ask most. Each page has one job, and each one ends with the same next step.

The five pages are a starting point, not a limit — extra pages cost $50 each, as published — but they are also a test. A business that cannot fill five honest pages does not need ten; it needs to say the five things well. A business that fills them easily knows exactly what the next pages should be, because its customers have already asked for them.

The questions before you hire

Before hiring any web designer, a Roswell business should ask four questions. Who owns the domain name, and is it registered in the business’s name? Who owns the files and the words? What does the price include, and what costs extra? And what happens if the business wants to leave — does the site go with it? A designer who answers all four clearly is a designer worth hiring.

The answers matter more than the portfolio. A beautiful portfolio built for businesses in other towns says nothing about whether this business will own its site, or whether the designer will answer the phone in six months. Ask the questions, write down the answers, and compare them across designers. The cheapest quote that cannot answer them is not a bargain.
