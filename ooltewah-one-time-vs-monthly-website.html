What an Ooltewah business actually needs

An Ooltewah business is usually small — often one person, or a small crew — and the website question is smaller than the sales pages make it. Most Ooltewah customers already know the business by name, or find it through a neighbour. The site needs to do three things: show the phone number where it can be tapped, say what you do in plain words, and name the area you serve. That is the whole job, and most Ooltewah sites need five pages at most to do it.

The site also needs to change rarely. A cleaning business, a repair business, a small shop — the services stay the same for months at a time, and the prices change less often than the sales pages suggest. That matters, because the right price depends on how often the site changes. A site that changes twice a year is a different purchase from a site that changes twice a week.

The three honest prices

The first price is nothing. Keep the phone number current in the places customers already look, and let word of mouth do the work. For a business with a full diary and no need for new customers, that is a reasonable choice — a website nobody visits is a cost with no return. The honest test is whether new customers are actually looking for you online. If they are, doing nothing costs more than it saves.

The second price is $500 once. WebCraft builds a five-page website for $500, one time, with $20-a-month hosting and care that is optional, not required. Extra pages are $50 each. You own the site, the domain and the files. The third price is a monthly plan: published monthly website prices in this market run around $99 to $130 a month, and one firm publishes a $4,000 one-time option. Hosting-only plans start at $9.99 a month, but they cover hosting and nothing else — no design, no pages, no care. Prices are published prices and they change; check the source before you decide.

The three-year total for a site that rarely changes

Put the three prices on the same basis, over three years, for a site that changes twice a year. A $500 one-time site with optional care at $20 a month costs $500, plus $720 of care over three years — $1,220 in total, or $500 if you arrange hosting yourself and skip the care. A $99-a-month plan costs $3,564 over three years. A $130-a-month plan costs $4,680. The gap is not small, and it comes from one fact: the monthly fee keeps charging for a site that is already built.

The monthly plan is not a bad product. It is a bad fit for a site that rarely changes. A business that updates its site twice a year is paying twelve monthly fees for two months of work. The one-time site fits that shape: pay for the build once, pay for care only in the months you use it, and keep the site in your hands either way.

When the monthly plan earns its keep

A monthly plan earns its keep when the site changes often. A business that changes prices every month, adds photos every week, or runs seasonal offers needs someone doing that work — and a monthly fee that includes edits is a fair price for it. The test is simple: count the changes you made to your site in the last six months. If the answer is two or three, the monthly plan is mostly rent. If the answer is twenty, it may be the cheapest way to get the work done.

Ask any monthly provider what the fee includes in a normal month: how many changes, what counts as extra, and what happens to the site if you cancel. A provider who answers with numbers is selling a service. A provider who answers with reassurance is selling a subscription. The difference shows up in year two, when the site is built and the monthly bill keeps coming.

The question that decides it

The decision comes down to two questions. How often will the site change? And who owns it when the payments stop? A $500 site you own, that changes twice a year, is the cheapest honest answer for most Ooltewah businesses. A monthly plan is the right answer when the site changes every week and you want someone else to handle it. Doing nothing is the right answer only when new customers are not looking for you online — and that is worth checking before you choose it.

Add up the three years, ask the ownership question in writing, and the right price is usually obvious.
