A Hixson Google Ads Starter Budget

A first Google Ads budget for a Hixson business should be a test, not a commitment. One service, one landing page, a small daily cap you can afford, and a month of honest tracking will tell you more than a big spend with no measure.

Start with one service, not the whole business

Hixson service businesses — plumbers, HVAC crews, lawn care, remodelers — serve Hixson, Soddy-Daisy and north Chattanooga from the same trucks. A starter campaign that advertises everything at once teaches nothing, because the business cannot tell which service brought the call. Pick the one service with the clearest demand and the best margin, and build the first campaign around that job alone.

The ad, the landing page and the phone call must agree on the same job. If the ad promises drain cleaning and the page talks about whole-house repiping, the visitor leaves. One service also keeps the budget honest: every dollar spent is a dollar spent learning whether that one service brings calls in this town.

Set a test budget you can afford to lose

The first budget is the price of the answer, not an investment with a promised return. Set a small daily cap — an amount the business can spend for a month without touching payroll, rent or fuel — and treat the month as a test. If the calls come, the budget grows because the numbers say so. If they do not, the business has bought a useful answer cheaply instead of an expensive lesson.

Never let a first campaign spend money the business needs elsewhere. A test that strains cash flow is not a test; it is a gamble, and a gamble makes the business quit before the numbers have had a month to speak. The right size is the size you can forget about while you do the actual work.

Send the ad to a page that does one job

The ad should land on a page built for the advertised service, not on the homepage. A Hixson customer searching on a phone along Hixson Pike wants three things in the first screen: the service, the area served, and a phone number a thumb can reach. A page that also sells three other services, a company history and a photo gallery is a page that asks the visitor to do the sorting.

The landing page is where a starter budget is won or lost. A good page turns a click into a call; a bad page turns the same click into a bounce, and the business blames the ads for what the page did. Build the page first, test it on your own phone, and only then pay for the clicks that land on it.

Choose the map before the keywords

Geography decides a starter budget more than keywords do. A Hixson business should show ads where its trucks actually go — Hixson and the nearby towns it serves — and exclude the places it will not drive to. Every click from a town you cannot serve is money spent on a customer you cannot take, and a small budget cannot afford many of those.

Negative keywords are the other half of the map: the words that mean the searcher is not a customer. “Free”, “DIY”, “jobs” and “how to” filter out the people who want to do the work themselves or find work, not hire it done. A short negative list, reviewed once a week in the first month, is the cheapest improvement a starter campaign gets.

Measure calls, not clicks

After a month, three numbers decide what happens next: how many real inquiries the ads brought, what each inquiry cost, and how many became booked jobs. Clicks, impressions and click-through rate are not on that list. A campaign with many clicks and no calls has spent money on nothing, and the business should know that in the first month, not the third.

Keep the decision simple. If the inquiries cover the spend and the jobs are good ones, keep the campaign and grow it slowly. If the inquiries are few or the wrong kind, change one thing — the page, the keywords, the geography — and test again. If nothing changes after an honest fix, stop. A starter budget that ends in a clear stop is still a success: it answered the question.
