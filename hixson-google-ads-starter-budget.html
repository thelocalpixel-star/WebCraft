A Hixson Google Ads Starter Budget

A first Google Ads budget for a Hixson business should be a test, not a commitment. One service, one landing page, a small daily cap you can afford, and a month of honest tracking will tell you more than a big spend with no measure.

Start with one service, not the whole business

Hixson service businesses — plumbers, HVAC crews, lawn care, remodelers — serve Hixson, Soddy-Daisy and north Chattanooga from the same trucks. A starter campaign that advertises everything at once teaches nothing, because the business cannot tell which service brought the call.

Pick the one service with the clearest demand and the best margin, and build the first campaign around that job alone.

The ad, the landing page and the phone call must agree on the same job. If the ad promises drain cleaning and the page talks about whole-house repiping, the visitor leaves. One service also keeps the budget honest: every dollar spent is a dollar spent learning whether that one service brings calls in this town.

Set a test budget you can afford to lose

Think of the first month’s spend as the cost of finding out, not as money that has to come back. Pick a daily cap the business can carry for a month without touching payroll, rent or fuel, and hold it steady.

If the calls come, the numbers argue for more. If they do not, the business has learned that cheaply.

Never fund the test with money the business needs for fuel, payroll or rent. A test that strains cash flow stops being a test: it becomes a gamble, and a gamble ends before the month of numbers is in. Size it so the business can forget about it and get on with the work.

Send the ad to a page that does one job

The ad should land on a page built for the advertised service, not on the homepage. A Hixson customer searching on a phone along Hixson Pike wants three things in the first screen: the service, the area served, and a phone number a thumb can reach.

A page that also sells three other services, a company history and a photo gallery is a page that asks the visitor to do the sorting.

For a Hixson business the page decides the budget, not the ad. A page that answers in the first screen turns a click into a call; a page that makes the visitor sort through services turns the same click into a bounce.

Build the page, test it on your own phone from Hixson Pike, and only then buy the clicks.

Choose the map before the keywords

Geography decides a starter budget more than keywords do. A Hixson business should show ads where its trucks actually go — Hixson and the nearby towns it serves — and exclude the places it will not drive to.

Every click from a town you cannot serve is money spent on a customer you cannot take, and a small budget cannot afford many of those.

The other half of the map is the words to exclude: the searches that mean the person is not a customer. “Free”, “DIY”, “jobs” and “how to” screen out the people who want to do the work themselves or find work, not hire it done.

Review that short list once a week in the first month — it is the cheapest fix a starter campaign gets.

Measure calls, not clicks

Give the test a month, then judge it on three numbers: real inquiries, what each one cost, and how many became booked jobs in Hixson, Soddy-Daisy or north Chattanooga. Clicks, impressions and click-through rate do not decide anything.

A campaign with many clicks and no calls has spent money on nothing — and the business should know that in month one, not month three.

The decision stays simple. If the inquiries cover the spend and the jobs are good ones, keep going and grow slowly. If they are few or the wrong kind, change one thing — the page, the map or the keywords — and test again.

If an honest fix changes nothing, stop. A test that ends in a clear stop still did its job: it answered the question.
