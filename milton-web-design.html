The questions to ask before hiring

Three questions matter before any money moves. What is the scope — which pages, and what does the price include. Who owns what — the domain name, the website files, the hosting account. And what happens after launch — who can change the site, and how.

The answers should be written down, in plain words, before the work starts, because a scope agreed in a phone call and forgotten by launch day is a scope that never existed.

The warning signs are the same in Milton as anywhere else: no written scope, the designer holding the domain in their own name, no handoff for the business to change its own site, and a price that cannot be explained in one sentence.

A designer who cannot answer the three questions plainly is a designer to skip — and a designer who answers them in writing, before being asked, is a designer worth talking to.

The five pages a Milton site needs first

Start a Milton site with five pages and nothing else. The homepage, which says what the business does, where in Milton it works, and how to reach it — a visitor arriving from an Atlanta search should know in seconds that this is a north Fulton business, not a downtown one.

A service page, in the customer’s words rather than the trade’s. An about page, saying in plain words who the business is. A contact page, carrying the phone number, the form, and the hours.

And one proof page — a real job, a real review in the customer’s own words — because an unproven claim gives the visitor no reason to believe it.

Five strong pages beat twenty thin ones. Every page answers one question, and the site can grow from there — another service page, a page for a nearby town, a page for the season.

The mistake is building the twenty first and never getting to the five that matter: a visitor who finds the answer on the first page they open picks up the phone, and a visitor who has to dig through filler clicks back to the results and calls the next business instead.

The first week after launch: what to check

Open the public site on a phone and try the contact paths. Send a labeled test inquiry, check that it reaches the right inbox, and confirm the opening hours and booking links are current.

Confirm the domain and hosting account access with the owner. Keep a short record of the tests and where each account is managed. Use Search Console to follow indexing once the site launches.
