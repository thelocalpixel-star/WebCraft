An Athens Business Details Accuracy Check

Check the business details Athens customers actually see — name, phone, hours and service area — and fix mismatches before any ranking work.

By WebCraft · Published November 5, 2026 · Local SEO accuracy check

Start with the list of places your details appear

Before you fix anything, write down every place a customer can find your business details. Start with your website and your business profile, if you have one. Then add the directory listings you control, your social media pages, your email signature, your invoices, and any printed material still in circulation. Most Athens businesses are surprised by how long this list gets, and that is exactly why the check matters: you cannot correct a detail in a place you forgot exists.

Work through the list one entry at a time and note what each one currently says. Do not rely on memory for this step. Open each listing and read it as a customer would, because a detail you set up years ago may no longer match the business you run today. A phone number from a previous handset, an old set of hours, or a trading name you stopped using can all sit quietly online for years, sending willing customers in the wrong direction.

Check the name customers actually use

Your business name is the first thing a searcher compares, so it has to match what customers already call you. If your legal name and your trading name differ, decide which one customers know and use that one consistently in the places they will see. A shortened version on your website, a formal version on a directory, and an abbreviation on a social page read as three different businesses, and a customer comparing results has no reason to work out that they are the same one.

Resist the temptation to add extra words to the name in the hope of being found more often. A name stuffed with services or place names looks wrong to a customer and is not a substitute for a clear service page. Keep the name exactly as customers know it, and let your services, your service area, and your reviews do the describing. Consistency here is a trust signal before it is anything else.

Phone and hours cost you customers first

Of all the details a customer sees, the phone number and the hours are the two that cost you business fastest when they are wrong. A number that rings unanswered, or one that belongs to a phone no one checks, ends the conversation before it starts. Hours that say you are open when you are not send someone on a wasted trip, and hours that say you are closed when you are open lose a customer who never calls at all.

Test the number yourself from another phone, and check what a caller hears when no one answers. Then read your hours the way a customer reads them: are they the hours you actually keep, including any early close or lunch break? If your hours change seasonally, or you close on certain days, say so plainly rather than leaving a generic schedule that is wrong half the year. These two fields are worth checking first because they are the ones a customer acts on immediately.

Fix mismatches from one written record

Once you have the full list, write down the correct version of each detail in one place: the exact business name, the phone number you answer, the hours you keep, and the area you serve in your own words. This single record becomes the reference for every correction. Without it, you will fix the website from memory, fix a directory from a different memory, and end up with two versions that still disagree.

Then work through your list and correct each entry to match the record, starting with the places customers see first: your website, your business profile, and the directories that appear when someone searches for your type of business in Athens. For listings you do not control, use whatever correction or claim process the directory provides, and note which ones you could not change so you can revisit them later. Keep the record somewhere the whole team can find it, so the next person who updates a detail starts from the same correct version.

Recheck after every change

Accuracy is not a one-time job. Every change to your business creates a new chance for details to drift: a new phone number, revised hours, a service you no longer offer, or a community you stopped serving. Each time something changes, update the written record first, then work through the list again. The recheck takes far less time than the first pass, because the list already exists and the correct version is already written down.

Set a simple routine around it. After any change, correct the details the same week. Once a quarter, spot-check the three places customers see most: your website, your business profile, and the first directory result for your type of business. Accurate details will not by themselves put a business at the top of any search result, and no one should promise that they will. But they are the foundation every other local search effort stands on, and they are the cheapest improvement a business can make.
