Planning a Website Project Remotely from Murphy

Organize files, reviews and ownership for a Murphy business working with a remote website designer.

Create a shared brief before discussing the draft

A remote project works better when the business goal is documented. A Murphy business should list its services, customers, operating area and preferred inquiry action. Include the current website if there is one and describe specific problems rather than relying only on visual preferences.

Put it into practice

Separate requirements from examples you simply like. Tell the designer what a reference communicates well without asking them to copy another company’s identity.

Keep reviews focused and attributable

Choose one person to collect feedback from the business. Review the draft against the agreed brief, marking factual corrections separately from visual preferences. Consolidated comments reduce conflicting directions and make the next revision easier to understand.

Put it into practice

For each requested change, identify the page, the issue and the intended customer benefit. Supply replacement copy when correcting a business fact.

Make launch and handoff visible milestones

Agree on the pages, revisions and tests before the project begins. Confirm domain access, hosting responsibilities, file ownership and form delivery before switching the public site. Online meetings do not remove the need for a concrete launch checklist.

Put it into practice

At handoff, review one normal content change and the support route for a problem. Keep account responsibilities in a business-controlled record.

Your planning checklist

Use these prompts to prepare a brief or review the work with your provider. Record the decision, the person responsible and the point at which it will be checked.

  • Brief → priority and requirement
  • Review → page, issue and proposed correction
  • Launch → access, testing and handoff owner

Resolve the items that affect a customer’s ability to understand the service or make contact first. Then agree on the remaining scope. A longer feature list is not automatically a better fit for your business.
