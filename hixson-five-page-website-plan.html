A Five-Page Website Plan for Hixson

WebCraft's standard website covers up to five pages for $500, one time. Five pages is enough for most Hixson businesses — if each page has a job, and if each page answers why Hixson, not Chattanooga. Hixson businesses compete with Chattanooga firms in search, and a Hixson page that reads like a Chattanooga page loses. This plan works through the five pages in order, and what to leave out until they work.

Hixson is not Chattanooga

Hixson businesses compete with Chattanooga firms in search, and that changes what the website must do. A customer searching in Hixson sees Chattanooga businesses in the results too — bigger firms, with more pages and more reviews. A Hixson site cannot win that comparison by pretending to be a Chattanooga firm. It wins by being the Hixson answer: the business that is here, that serves Hixson, and that a Hixson customer can reach today.

Say Hixson first, on the home page, in the first screen. Name the service and the place together: what you do, and that you do it in Hixson. Do not bury Hixson in a list of towns at the bottom of the page, and do not claim Chattanooga neighbourhoods you cannot reach. The customer who searches in Hixson wants a Hixson business. Give them one.

Page one: home — say Hixson first

The home page has one offer and one next step. The offer is the service and the place, in one line: what you do, in Hixson. The next step is a phone call, or a quote request — one action, not three. The first screen should carry the phone number, tappable, and the service list below it. No slideshow, no stock photograph of a skyline, no paragraph about the company's mission.

Test the home page on your phone, the way a customer would. Can you find the phone number without scrolling? Can you read what the business does in five seconds? Can you tell it serves Hixson? If any answer is no, fix the home page before adding a sixth page. A home page that fails the five-second test fails for every visitor, whatever the rest of the site contains.

Page two: services — one service, one clear offer

The services page lists what you do, in the customer's words, one service at a time. Each service gets a short explanation — what it is, who it is for, what happens next — and nothing else. A plumber lists water heaters, leaks and installations as three separate offers, not as one paragraph called “plumbing services”. A cleaner lists regular cleaning, deep cleaning and move-out cleaning as three offers, each with its own next step.

One service, one clear offer, and one next step per service. The page should not try to sell the whole business; it should help a visitor with one problem find the one service that solves it. If a service has no next step — no call, no quote, no booking — it does not belong on the page yet. Add the step first, then the service.

Page three: service area — the towns you actually reach

The service-area page names Hixson and the towns you genuinely serve, and stops there. A Hixson business that serves the nearby communities should name them, accurately, with a sentence each on what you do there. A business that lists every town in the county is not being thorough; it is making a promise it cannot keep, and the customers from those towns will find out.

The area page also decides what the site is found for. A page that names Hixson and the towns around it, accurately, is a page that can be found by people searching in those towns. A page that claims everywhere convinces nobody and ranks for nothing. Write the area once, honestly, and spend the rest of the site on the work itself.

Pages four and five: about and contact

The about page is proof, not biography. Who you are, how long you have worked in Hixson, and what you do — in a few sentences, with a real photo of real work if you have one. No invented reviews, no borrowed testimonials, no claims about awards or rankings that cannot be checked. A short honest about page beats a long invented one, because a customer who meets you in person will know the difference.

The contact page is the phone call. A tappable phone number at the top, the hours you actually keep, and a form with as few fields as possible: name, phone, and what they need. Every extra field is a reason to stop. What to leave out until the five pages work: a blog, a photo gallery, a second service-area page, and any page that exists because a competitor has one. Add those only when the five pages are bringing calls — the site earns its sixth page, it does not start with one.
