A Website Handoff Checklist for Maryville Businesses

Plan a website handoff for your Maryville business before launch. Record domain ownership, website files, form delivery and GA4 account access so website maintenance and future changes have a clear owner.

By WebCraft · Published November 4, 2026 · Practical business guide

Define the deliverables in plain words

Ownership can mean different things depending on the platform and agreement. Ask what files, content and accounts you receive, what requires a subscription and what can move to another provider. For a Maryville business, the right handoff makes ordinary changes and future decisions understandable.

Put it into practice

Add a deliverables list to the proposal. Distinguish a copy of the website from access to the account that currently hosts it.

Check accounts that the website relies on

Domain registration, DNS, hosting, forms and analytics may all use separate accounts. Clarify who controls each one and where renewal notices go. Access should be assigned to the appropriate business users through the provider’s supported process. Avoid passing shared credentials through public project comments.

Put it into practice

Record the owner and purpose of each account without including secrets. Identify dependencies such as a form subscription or an external booking tool.

Practice one ordinary update

Before closing the project, ask how to change an hour, replace an image or request support. The answer should match the agreed care plan. Check what happens when the business changes providers and whether any export restrictions apply.

Put it into practice

Keep a short handoff document with current contact routes, account responsibilities and renewal dates. Review it when the business changes staff or the website changes platform.

Your planning checklist

Use these prompts to prepare a brief or review the work with your provider. Record the decision, the person responsible and the point at which it will be checked.

  • Deliverable → file, content or account access
  • Renewal → payer and notification recipient
  • Routine update → process and responsibility

Resolve the items that affect a customer’s ability to understand the service or make contact first. Then agree on the remaining scope. A longer feature list is not automatically a better fit for your business.
