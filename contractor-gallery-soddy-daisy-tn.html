Building a Useful Project Gallery for a Soddy-Daisy Contractor

A contractor website portfolio should explain the work a customer can request. Plan project-gallery photos, permissions and captions for a Soddy-Daisy business website before choosing the gallery layout.

By WebCraft · Published November 4, 2026 · Practical business guide

Select examples for relevance rather than volume

A gallery should help someone understand the jobs you take on. A Soddy-Daisy contractor can begin with a small selection representing core services instead of publishing every photograph from a project folder. Choose images that make the work visible and retain enough context to explain it accurately.

Put it into practice

Write the service demonstrated beside each candidate image. If the image cannot be connected to the offer, it may not earn its place on the page.

Write captions that establish context

Explain the project type and the work your business actually performed. Do not imply responsibility for an entire job when your role was limited. Obtain publication permission and avoid sharing identifiable addresses without a reason and authorization. A factual caption makes an image more useful to a potential customer.

Put it into practice

For before-and-after pairs, verify that the images depict the same project and that both can be published. Do not manufacture a transformation by pairing unrelated photos.

Connect the gallery to the next decision

A visitor who recognizes a relevant project should be able to learn about that service and make contact. Use descriptive links and a clear inquiry option beside the gallery. Keep images appropriately sized and check their crops on a phone so the important work remains visible.

Put it into practice

Ask someone unfamiliar with the project to identify the service shown. If the answer is unclear, improve the caption or choose a more informative image.

Your planning checklist

Use these prompts to prepare a brief or review the work with your provider. Record the decision, the person responsible and the point at which it will be checked.

  • Image → service demonstrated
  • Caption → factual role and permission
  • Next step → relevant service and inquiry

Resolve the items that affect a customer’s ability to understand the service or make contact first. Then agree on the remaining scope. A longer feature list is not automatically a better fit for your business.
