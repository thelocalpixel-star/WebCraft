Google Ads vs Facebook Ads in Cleveland

A Cleveland business choosing between Google Ads and Facebook Ads is choosing between two kinds of attention. Google catches people who are already searching for the service; Facebook interrupts people who are scrolling and might want it. Both can work, and both can waste money — the difference is which kind of attention the business needs, and what each inquiry actually costs.

Intent vs interest

Google is intent. The person typing Cleveland plumber, roof repair near me, or kitchen remodel Cleveland has already decided they need the work — they are choosing who does it, not whether to do it. That searcher is the most valuable visitor a local business can buy, because they arrive ready to hire, and the only question is whether the ad and the page answer them first.

Facebook is interest. The person scrolling is not looking for a plumber or a remodeler; they are looking at friends, photos, and news, and the ad has to earn a second of their attention before it earns a click. That visitor may be months from hiring, or may never hire at all — but they are cheaper to reach, and there are more of them. Intent is a smaller pool of hotter water; interest is a larger pool, cooler, and further from the tap.

What the 2025 benchmarks actually say

Published 2025 benchmarks put Google search ads near $5.26 per click and about $70 per lead, against about $1.72 per click and $25 to $30 per lead on Meta. Those are averages across many industries and many markets — not promises for Cleveland, and not prices for any one business — but the shape of the gap is the point: a Google click costs roughly three times a Meta click, and a Google lead costs roughly two to three times a Meta lead.

The gap exists because the searcher is different. Google charges more because the person typing is closer to hiring, and the businesses bidding for that person know it. Meta charges less because the person scrolling may never hire, and the businesses bidding for their attention know that too. A benchmark is a starting point for a test, not a budget — and any provider that quotes these numbers as a guarantee for a Cleveland business is selling a number it does not own.

Why the cheaper click is not the cheaper customer

A cheaper click is not a cheaper customer, because a click is not a customer. A Meta campaign can win hundreds of clicks at $1.72 each and bring in no inquiries, if the people clicking were curious rather than ready. A Google campaign can win twenty clicks at $5.26 each and bring in three calls, if the people clicking were ready to hire. The business that judges the two by cost per click will pick the cheaper one and wonder why the phone stays quiet.

The difference shows up in the follow-up, too. A Google inquiry usually arrives with a job attached — a leak, a broken roof, a kitchen to remodel — and the business can quote it the same day. A Meta inquiry often arrives as a name and a number with a vague interest, and the business has to do the selling: call back, qualify, follow up, and wait. The cheaper lead that needs three calls to become a job can cost more than the expensive lead that becomes a job on the first call.

Which one fits which Cleveland business

Google fits the business people search for in a hurry: the plumber, the electrician, the roofer, the locksmith, the emergency repair — the jobs where the customer types the service and the town and calls the first business that answers. In Cleveland, where the searches are fewer than in Chattanooga but the searcher is just as ready, that urgency is the whole value of the ad.

Facebook fits the business people discover slowly: the remodeler with before-and-after photos, the restaurant with a new menu, the real estate agent with a new listing, the shop with a seasonal offer — the businesses where a photo or a story does the selling, and the customer needs to see it three times before they act. A Cleveland remodeler showing a finished kitchen in a Paul Huff Parkway subdivision is selling a vision, not answering a search, and Facebook is where that vision is seen.

Cost per inquiry is the decider

The decision is not made by the benchmarks; it is made by the business's own numbers. Run one small test on each — one service, one page, one month, a budget the business can afford to lose — and count the real inquiries each one brings, what each inquiry cost, and how many became booked jobs. The platform with the lower cost per inquiry wins, for that service, in this town, this season.

And the answer can change. A roofing business may find Google wins in storm season and Facebook wins for remodels. A restaurant may find Facebook wins for weekend traffic and Google wins for catering inquiries. Test both, count inquiries and not clicks, and let the numbers decide — and retest every season, because the answer that was right in January may be wrong in July, and the business that keeps testing is the business that keeps its ad money working.
