How Much Does a Website Cost in Dayton?

Dayton is the county seat of Rhea County, a town of 7,065 people in a county of 32,870. It is also a town where no local web firm publishes a website price, which makes comparing costs harder than it should be. This guide puts the honest numbers side by side: WebCraft's price first, then the published prices from firms nearby, and the three-year totals that show what each option really costs.

WebCraft's price first

WebCraft charges $500 one time for a website, with optional hosting and care at $20 a month. Extra pages are $50 each.

Those are the published prices, and they are the whole price list: there is no setup fee beyond the $500, and the $20 a month is optional, not required — a business that already has hosting it is happy with can take the website without the monthly care.

The $500 covers a five-page website built for the business, with the pages written around the services the business actually sells and the towns it actually serves.

The three-year total for the full package is easy to work out, because both numbers are published: $500 once, plus $20 a month for thirty-six months, is $1,220 over three years. Without the monthly care, the total is $500, and the business looks after its own hosting and updates.

Either way the business owns the website, and either way the price for the next three years is known on the day the project starts. That is the comparison every other price in this guide should be measured against: not just the first number, but the total over three years, with the monthly cost included.

The other published prices nearby

No Dayton, Tennessee web firm publishes a website price, so the comparison has to come from firms nearby that do publish. Trueline, a regional Tennessee firm, publishes builds from $750 to $2,500, with care plans from $49 to $179 a month.

East Tennessee Web Creator, based in East Tennessee and building for businesses anywhere in the United States, publishes a $200 Starter package and Small Business packages from $400. Sullivan Web Design, run by an MTSU student in Murfreesboro, Tennessee, publishes a $500 build with hosting and updates at $35 a month.

Those are the published numbers, as published in November 2026, and they are ranges, not quotes: Trueline's build price depends on the size of the site, and East Tennessee Web Creator's Small Business price starts at $400 and rises with scope.

A range is not a promise of a price for any particular business, and a business comparing these firms should ask for a written quote for its own site before deciding.

What the published numbers do show is the shape of the market: a build price and, in most cases, a monthly price beside it — and it is the monthly price, paid every month for years, that decides the real cost.

What the price does not tell you

A build price does not say who owns the website, what happens if the business wants to move, or what the monthly price actually covers.

Ask each firm, in writing: who owns the domain and the website files, what the monthly price includes — updates, backups, support, or only the hosting itself — and what happens to the site if the business stops paying.

A cheap build with a monthly price that covers almost nothing can cost more over three years than a higher build with care included, and a site the business does not own is not an asset, whatever it cost.

Also ask what the renewal price is, against the first year's price. Some monthly prices rise after the first year, and the price a business agrees to in the first month is not always the price it pays in the thirteenth.

And ask what is included in the build: how many pages, who writes the words, who supplies the photos, and what happens if the business wants a page changed after launch. The answers to those questions are worth more than the build price on its own, because they describe the next three years, not the next three weeks.

How to choose in Dayton

The way to choose is to compare the same scope, not just the first number. Write down what the business needs — the pages, the services, the towns served — and ask each firm for a written quote for that scope, with the monthly price and the three-year total beside it.

A quote that will not state the monthly price, or that will not put the total in writing, is a quote that cannot be compared, and a business should be wary of deciding on a number that only covers the first month.

For a Dayton business, the practical choice is usually between the lowest honest total and the firm the business trusts to look after the site. The published numbers say the lowest total with hosting and care included is WebCraft's $1,220 over three years, and the next is Sullivan's $1,760.

But a price is not a recommendation: the business should talk to the firm, read its work, and ask the questions in the previous section before deciding. A website is a three-year relationship, not a one-time purchase, and the right choice is the one the business can live with for three years, at a total it knew before it started.
