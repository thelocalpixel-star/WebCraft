A Phone-First Website Plan for Sevierville

A Sevierville business does not get most of its calls from people planning a trip. It gets them from visitors who are already in town, already on the road, and deciding right now whether to drive over. A phone-first website is built for that caller: the number first, the three questions answered before the call, and nothing in the way. This plan covers what the site should do for a tourist who is deciding in the next two minutes.

The tourist call: already in town, deciding now

A visitor in Sevierville is not browsing websites the way a local plans a project. They are in the car, or standing on a sidewalk, comparing two or three places on a phone. The decision takes two minutes, and the business that answers first usually wins it. A website that makes a visitor read three paragraphs before finding the number is a website that loses to the business whose number is at the top of the page.

The plan starts from that caller. Every page should put the phone number where a thumb can reach it without scrolling, and the number should be a link that starts the call. A number written as plain text on a phone is a number the visitor has to copy by hand, and a visitor who has to copy a number will tap the next result instead.

Answer three questions before the call button

A tourist asks three questions before calling: are you open now, how much does it cost, and how do I get there. The website should answer all three above the call button, in plain words. “Open today until 9pm. Tickets from $18. Two miles from the Parkway, free parking.” A visitor who has those answers calls with a decision already made; a visitor who does not has to ask, and may not bother.

The answers should be current, not general. “Open daily” is not an answer when the business closes early on a Sunday or shuts for a private event. The site should say what is true today, and the business should update it when today changes. A tourist who drives over on the strength of an out-of-date answer does not come back.

Hours today, not hours in general

Seasonal hours are the most common reason a Sevierville visitor is disappointed. A business that keeps summer hours in July and shorter hours in January needs a website that says which season it is in, not a single set of hours that is wrong half the year. The hours on the homepage should be the hours for this week, and the business should change them when the season changes.

The same rule applies to closures. A day closed for a holiday, a private booking, or a storm should be on the website before the visitor drives over, not discovered at a locked door. One honest line — “Closed Monday, January 12 for a private event” — saves a trip and keeps the customer for the next visit.

Make the number work from a moving car

A phone-first site is tested in a car, not at a desk. Open the site on a phone, in sunlight, with one hand. Can you find the number in two seconds. Can you tap it without zooming. Does the call start, or does the page ask you to copy the number first. If any of those fail, the site is not phone-first, whatever it calls itself.

The test also covers the pages behind the homepage. A visitor who lands on a service page from a search should find the number there too, not have to go back to the homepage. Every page is a front door for a tourist who found it through search, and every front door needs a number on it.

The call you miss is the customer you lose

A missed call in Sevierville is not a missed call in a town where customers plan ahead. A tourist who does not get through calls the next business on the list, and that business is two minutes away. The website can bring the call, but only the business can answer it — and the hours the phone is answered should be the hours the website promises.

Measure the calls, not the visits. A site that brings a hundred visits and no calls is a site that looks good and sells nothing. A site that brings ten calls a day is a site that works, even if the visit count is small. The number to watch is answered calls, and the fix for a low number is usually the phone, not the website.
