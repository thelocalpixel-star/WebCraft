What Local SEO Costs in Maryville, TN

A Maryville business asking what local SEO costs gets two kinds of answers: a number with no explanation, or a refusal to give a number at all. The honest answer is a range, with the reasons for it. This guide gives the published 2026 ranges, what each band buys, why the price moves, and how to compare two quotes without comparing only the price.

The range, stated first

The published 2026 ranges agree on the shape. WebFX’s 2026 survey of 250 US businesses, as published in November 2026, puts local SEO retainers at $500 to $3,000 a month, with small businesses spending around $1,000 a month.

Ahrefs’ survey of 439 providers, as published in November 2026, found local SEO averaging $1,557 a month, with the most common band $501 to $1,000 — the band about one provider in five charges. One-time projects run $500 to $5,000, on the same WebFX figures.

Those are national ranges, and a single-location business in a smaller market should expect the lower half, not the top. A Maryville business is not buying a campaign for a big city with dozens of competitors for every search — it is buying one location done properly, and the price should reflect that.

A quote at the top of the national range for a single Maryville location is a quote that needs explaining.

What each band actually buys

Under $500 a month usually buys software, listing tools, or a few hours of work — enough to keep a Business Profile tidy, and rarely enough to move up in a busy area. It is a start, not a programme, and a business paying it should know which one it is buying.

The work at this level is mostly maintenance: the profile kept current, the listings kept consistent, and a report that says what happened.

From $500 to $1,000 a month buys one location done properly: profile optimisation, citation cleanup, local keyword targeting, on-page fixes, and a monthly report. From $1,000 to $2,500 a month adds location pages, regular content, review growth and local links — the work a business needs when the market is competitive, or when it serves more than one town.

The band is not the quality; the work inside the band is.

Why the price moves: scope, market, starting point

Three things move a quote. The scope — how much work there is: how many pages, how many services, how many locations. The market — how competitive the searches are, and how many businesses are chasing the same customers.

And the starting point — where the business begins: a new site with no pages and no reviews needs more work than an established site with both, and the first months are always the heaviest.

A Maryville business should ask any quote to name those three things. How many pages, which listings, which reviews, what reports — and what the first three months include, because the audit, the fixes and the first pages all land in the first months.

A quote that cannot say what it covers is a quote that cannot be compared, and a quote that cannot be compared is a quote that cannot be trusted.

Retainer, one-time, or hourly

The retainer is the most common model: a fixed monthly fee for a set of tasks or hours. Most agencies bill this way, and most businesses buy this way, because local SEO is ongoing work — pages to write, listings to keep right, reviews to earn, results to measure.

A retainer should name the tasks, the hours, and the report, so the business can see what the month bought.

One-time projects suit a single job: an audit, a citation cleanup, a profile setup, a set of pages. Hourly suits a question, not a programme — an hour of advice is worth buying, and a year of hourly billing is a retainer without the name.

The model matters less than the scope: whatever the model, the business should know what work happens, who does it, and how it will be measured — in inquiries, not in positions.

How to compare two quotes without comparing only the price

Put the quotes side by side with the scope next to the price — what pages, what listings, what reviews, what reports — and compare the work, not the number. Two quotes at the same monthly fee can be entirely different scopes, and two quotes at different fees can be the same work with different labels.

The quote that lists the work can be judged. The quote that lists only a number cannot.

Ask both companies the same four questions: what do you do each month, how do you measure it, who owns the website and the profile, and what happens if we stop. The quote that answers all four is the quote to trust.

WebCraft’s own position is stated plainly: local SEO is quoted around the market — the scope, the starting point and the competition decide the number, and no fixed price is published here, because none exists to publish.
