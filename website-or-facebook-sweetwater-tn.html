Website or Facebook Page? A Sweetwater Business Checklist

Decide how a business website and Facebook page can work together, with a clear plan for information and ownership.

By WebCraft · Published November 4, 2026 · Practical business guide

Give the channels different jobs

A social page can support updates and conversations, while a website can organize stable information around your services. A Sweetwater business deciding on a first site should start with what customers struggle to find today. There is no need to duplicate every post into a new webpage.

Put it into practice

List your most important information and where it currently lives. Check whether a new visitor can find it without scrolling through older updates.

Make service information easy to revisit

A focused website can give each important offer a durable address. Include current contact details, a clear explanation and a relevant next step. Keep the social profile connected to that information. If you use both channels, assign an owner so the two do not contradict each other.

Put it into practice

Choose which channel is authoritative for hours, prices where published and service boundaries. Update linked information when those facts change.

Compare costs and control before committing

A domain, hosting account and website files have different responsibilities. Clarify who controls them and what ongoing work costs. A social account has its own platform rules and access arrangements. Understand both rather than assuming one channel replaces every function of the other.

Put it into practice

Write down the smallest website scope that solves the information problem. Add features only when they support a process the business can maintain.

Your planning checklist

Use these prompts to prepare a brief or review the work with your provider. Record the decision, the person responsible and the point at which it will be checked.

  • Customer question → best place for the answer
  • Shared fact → owner and update process
  • Website scope → useful page or unnecessary feature

Resolve the items that affect a customer’s ability to understand the service or make contact first. Then agree on the remaining scope. A longer feature list is not automatically a better fit for your business.
