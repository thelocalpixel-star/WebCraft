A practical guide for East Tennessee businesses

What does a small business website cost?

A useful website can be as simple as a few pages that explain what you do and help customers contact you. The right budget depends on what those pages need to do—and who will look after them.

If you own a business in Cleveland, Chattanooga, Athens, Knoxville, or nearby, use this guide to compare quotes and understand the costs that can continue after launch.

Start with the job your website needs to do

A contractor may need a service list, project photos, and a quote form. A café might need an up-to-date menu, hours, and directions. A shop with online payments or inventory will need more than a simple informational site.

Write down your three most important customer actions—such as calling, requesting an estimate, or finding your address. That list gives a designer a clear starting point and helps you avoid paying for features you won’t use.

What changes the price?

  • Number of pages and content: A small site with a few service pages generally takes less work than a large catalog. Ask who writes the text and supplies photographs.
  • Custom features: Booking, online payments, customer accounts, and inventory can add setup and ongoing costs. Ask whether they are included or supplied by a third party.
  • Revisions and updates: Confirm how many changes are included before launch and what happens when your hours, prices, or services change later.
  • Domain and hosting: Your domain is your web address; hosting makes the site available online. These may be billed separately or included in a care plan.
  • Ownership: Ask who controls the domain, website files, and third-party accounts, and what you receive if you move to another provider.

Questions to ask before you accept a quote

  1. What pages, contact methods, and mobile layouts are included?
  2. Are the domain, hosting, and any third-party subscriptions included? What do they cost after launch?
  3. Who will provide the writing and images, and do we have permission to use them?
  4. What revisions are included, and how are future updates handled?
  5. Will I own the website files and have access to the domain and relevant accounts?

There isn’t one price that fits every business. Compare proposals by the work and ongoing costs they actually cover, then choose the option that fits the way customers will use your site.
