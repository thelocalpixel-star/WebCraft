Planning Five Website Pages for a Cookeville Business

Give each page a clear job before choosing a five-page website design package for your Cookeville business. Plan the service descriptions, business information and inquiry form around what customers need.

Assign a purpose to every page

A five-page package is a limit on scope, not a requirement to fill space. A Cookeville business might need a homepage, services, examples, about and contact. Another may need two distinct service pages instead of a gallery. Choose pages according to the information a buyer needs.

Put it into practice

Write the visitor question beside each proposed page. If a page has no meaningful answer of its own, reconsider it rather than padding it with broad marketing language.

Keep the homepage focused on the introduction

The homepage should establish what the company does and guide people to the right detail. It does not have to contain every paragraph from the rest of the site. Use concise summaries and descriptive links that continue a useful task.

Put it into practice

Prepare a simple navigation with labels a new customer understands. Explain important offers before asking someone to choose between unfamiliar service names.

Agree on the boundary of the package

Clarify how page count is determined and which content or features are included. Booking, payments and unusual integrations can change the scope. WebCraft’s current package is $500 for up to five pages; additional pages are $50 each, with hosting and third-party charges handled separately.

Put it into practice

List the planned pages and requirements in the written quote. A clear boundary helps you compare proposals and avoid discovering missing work at launch.

Your planning checklist

Use these prompts to prepare a brief or review the work with your provider. Record the decision, the person responsible and the point at which it will be checked.

  • Page → customer question
  • Navigation → clear label and destination
  • Scope → content, features and separate costs

Resolve the items that affect a customer’s ability to understand the service or make contact first. Then agree on the remaining scope. A longer feature list is not automatically a better fit for your business.
