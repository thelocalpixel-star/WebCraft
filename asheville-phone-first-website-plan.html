A Phone-First Website Plan for Asheville

An Asheville customer rarely calls the first business they find. They compare two or three small shops — a roofer, a plumber, a landscaper — and call the one that makes it easiest. A phone-first website is built for that comparison: the number first, the question answered before the call, and the person who answers named on the page. This plan covers what the site should do for a caller who is choosing between three shops.

The comparison call

A caller comparing three Asheville shops is not looking for the best website. They are looking for the easiest call to make, and the easiest call is the one where the number is at the top of the page, the question is already answered, and the person who answers is named. A website that makes a visitor read three paragraphs before finding the number is a website that loses to the shop whose number is first.

The comparison also happens fast. A caller who has three tabs open will call the first number they can find and the first business that answers. The site’s job is to be the first number found; the phone’s job is to be the first one answered. A phone-first plan covers both, because a site that brings calls the business does not answer is a site that works for the competitor.

The number first, before the story

The phone number should be the first thing on the page after the headline, on every page, and it should be a link that starts the call. A number written as plain text on a phone is a number the visitor has to copy by hand, and a visitor comparing three shops will not copy a number — they will tap the next result. The number should also be big enough to tap with a thumb, without zooming, in sunlight, with one hand.

The story comes after the number, not before it. A visitor who wants the story will read it; a visitor who wants to call should not have to. The homepage of a small Asheville business is not a magazine — it is a front door, and the number is the handle.

Answer the question the caller is really asking

A caller comparing shops is really asking one question: can you do my job, and what will it cost me to find out. The website should answer both before the call. “We repair and replace roofs in Asheville and Buncombe County. Call for a free estimate, usually the same day.” That is the answer. A page that says “contact us for more information” is a page that has not answered anything.

The answer should also say what the call will be like. “When you call, we will ask what the work is, where it is, and when you need it done — two minutes, and you will have a price or a time for a visit.” A caller who knows what the call involves is a caller who makes it. A caller who does not know is a caller who keeps comparing.

Name the person who answers

A small shop has an advantage a big company does not: the person who answers the phone is the person who does the work, or the person who books it. Name them. “Call and ask for Sam — he answers the phone and books the visits.” A named person is a reason to call this shop instead of the next one, and it costs nothing to add.

The name should be real, and the person named should be the one who answers. A named person who never picks up is worse than no name at all, because the caller asked for someone and was told they are not there. The phone-first plan is honest about who answers, when they answer, and what happens when they do not.

Missed calls are the metric that matters

A phone-first site is measured in answered calls, not visits. A site that brings a hundred visits and no calls is a site that looks good and sells nothing. A site that brings ten calls a day is a site that works, even if the visit count is small. The number to watch is answered calls, and the fix for a low number is usually the phone, not the website.

The missed calls deserve a count of their own. A business that misses five calls a week is losing five customers a week to the shop that answered. The fix is practical: an answering message that says when the business will call back, and a call back within the hour, every time. A website can bring the call, but only the business can keep it.
